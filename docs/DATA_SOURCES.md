# Data sources and science references

The game uses real space weather records to give players a concrete reason to make decisions. It also uses orbital data to estimate the radio delay between Earth and Mars. Each playable mission is a fictional simulation with its own resource and scheduling rules.

## Data used by the game

| Source | What the game takes from it | Where it appears |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [NASA CCMC Space Weather Database of Notifications, Knowledge, Information (DONKI)](https://ccmc.gsfc.nasa.gov/tools/DONKI/) | Event type, date, identifier, source URL, and available details such as a flare’s class or a CME’s speed | Solar alert cards and the mission report |
| [NASA/JPL approximate planetary positions](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/planets/approx_pos.html) | Approximate orbital elements for Earth and Mars | The estimate of how long a radio message takes to travel between the planets |

## From a DONKI record to an alert

The browser queries NASA CCMC’s DONKI web service. The client requests solar flares (FLR), coronal mass ejection analyses (CME), solar energetic particle events (SEP), and geomagnetic storms (GST). It converts the records to a common format and maps them to gameplay strengths from one to three. Source dates and links stay with the records. A strength score is a teaching and game mechanic. For example, the current client assigns SEP records a strength of two.

Mission setup offers three ways to build the pool:

- **Surprise** combines the past 30 days with a randomly chosen historical week. The bundled archive can fill missing event types.
- **This week** asks for the past seven days. If the query returns no usable records, the mission uses the archive.
- **Famous storms** asks for a selected historical period. The game prefers records from the same period in the bundled archive if the service returns nothing. The general archive is a further fallback.

Records fetched for the recent window can carry the live-data badge. That label refers to the data source and time window. Historical and archived records show the real event date. The mission’s alert order and the crew’s experience are generated by the game.

### Example used in the demo

A solar flare dated **December 14, 2023** becomes a radio alert. Playing the backup antenna choice keeps the radio working and spends three energy for one science point in the simulation. The date and original record come from DONKI. The outpost, radio outcome, and resource changes are gameplay. [Archived record in the repository](../src/data/donki.ts)

## Other references and their role

The in-game sources page offers science references for further reading. OSDR, RadLab, Mars Trek, and the Canadian Space Agency’s food material are resource links. The runtime data path described above reads DONKI and calculates an orbital estimate from built-in NASA/JPL elements.

- [NASA Mars facts](https://science.nasa.gov/mars/facts/) covers a Martian day, seasons, moons, and the atmosphere.
- [NASA’s MOXIE experiment](https://www.nasa.gov/solar-system/nasas-oxygen-generating-experiment-moxie-completes-mars-mission/) demonstrates extraction of oxygen from the Martian atmosphere.
- [Water recovery on the International Space Station](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/station/iss-research/nasa-achieves-water-recovery-milestone-on-international-space-station/) informs the water-recovery lesson.
- [NASA/JPL Phoenix water ice](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/nasa-phoenix-mars-lander-confirms-frozen-water/) informs the buried-ice event.
- [Radishes harvested on the ISS](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/station/astronauts-harvest-radish-crop-on-international-space-station/) informs the greenhouse harvest. [CSA food production resources](https://www.asc-csa.gc.ca/eng/sciences/food-production/)
- [NASA Open Science Data Repository](https://osdr.nasa.gov/) and [NASA RadLab](https://visualization.osdr.nasa.gov/radlab/) provide background on space biology and radiation.
- [NASA Mars Trek](https://trek.nasa.gov/mars/) provides public tools and maps for exploring Mars.

See the [original research log in Portuguese](DATA_SOURCES.pt-BR.md) for the complete card-level reference inventory and its verification status.

## Simulation units

The outpost’s energy, air, food, science, and health are game units. The panel output, daily consumption, radiation damage, effectiveness of the sand shield, greenhouse harvest schedules, and event effects are balance values. The radio delay is a rounded one-way estimate from the approximate Earth–Mars distance using the speed of light. [Balance code](../src/game/engine/balance.ts)
